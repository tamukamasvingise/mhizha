# VAT in Zimbabwe

Researched 2026-11-25. General research, not tax advice. **An accountant or ZIMRA must confirm the points under "Unknowns" before a Business registers.**

## Short answer

- **Before registration, charge and show no VAT.** ZIMRA: "Only operators properly registered for Value Added Tax (VAT) purposes may charge VAT" [Z1]. Saying tax is included, or adding it, where none is payable is an offence (s 62(1)(f)) [A]. Issue an ordinary Invoice, never a "tax invoice".
- **Not being registered does not remove the liability.** The Act taxes anyone who "is or is required to be registered" (s 2, "registered operator") [A]. If a Business should have registered, ZIMRA can backdate it, and prices are treated as already including VAT (s 69).
- **Registration is compulsory** once taxable supplies in the last 12 months exceed US$25,000, or are reasonably expected to exceed it in the next 12 months. Apply within 30 days.
- **After registration, the time of supply decides VAT, not simply the Invoice date.** Normally it is the earliest of: Invoice issued; payment received; goods removed, or immovable goods handed over; service performed (s 8(1)).
- **Invoices that carry VAT:** those for standard-rated supplies whose time of supply is on or after the registration date. Current rate: **15.5%** [Z2].
- **Invoices that do not (best reading):** those issued before registration, even if paid later, and work fully finished before registration but invoiced later. An unapplied **Deposit** is not a payment, so a Deposit held at registration does not protect the later Invoice from VAT.
- **Work under way and stage payments are unclear.** I found no section written for a business that registers later. Get a ruling or an accountant's decision for each affected Job.
- After registration, "tax invoice" means a **fiscal** tax invoice from a compatible fiscal device or software, transmitted to ZIMRA's FDMS. An ordinary PDF is not enough.

## Findings

### 1. Before registration

- VAT falls on a "registered operator" (s 6(1)(a)), but that includes someone **required** to be registered (s 2). [A]
- s 62(1)(f) makes it an offence for a supplier to declare that tax "has been included in or will be added to the price", "where in fact no tax is payable", or without reasonable cause to add such tax. [A]
- Tender exception: since 1 January 2025, s 70(2) says a price quoted by a person who is **not** registered, for taxable supplies "with an aggregate tender value exceeding the amount specified in section 23(1)", "shall include tax in the tender-quoted price". [F24] *Inference:* this concerns tender quotes; it is not permission to collect VAT on ordinary Invoices. Its fit with s 62 is unclear, so ask before any tender over US$25,000.
- VAT paid on purchases cannot be claimed back while unregistered.

### 2. When registration is compulsory

- **Looking back**, s 23(1)(a): liable "at the end of any month" if taxable supplies in the 12 months ending then exceeded US$25,000. This is a rolling 12 months, checked monthly, not a calendar or tax year. [A, F23]
- **Looking ahead**, s 23(1)(b): liable at the start of any month when there are reasonable grounds to believe the next 12 months will exceed it. One large signed Job can trigger this. [A]
- "Exceeds" means exactly US$25,000 does not trigger the Act's test. ZIMRA's wording is looser ("exceed or are likely to reach") [Z2].
- Count supplies without tax (ss 9(2), 23(6)). Include standard-rated and zero-rated; leave out exempt (s 2, "taxable supply"). Labour and parts both count: turnover, not profit. [A]
- *Inference:* count by time of supply, so unpaid Invoices count and unapplied Deposits do not yet.
- The Commissioner may disregard an excess caused **solely** by winding down, replacing plant or capital assets, or temporary abnormal circumstances (s 23(1) proviso). It is not automatic. [A]
- Splitting one trade between people can be undone by a direction (s 52). [A]
- Local-currency sales are converted at an equivalent rate, but the texts differ: [A] says the prevailing rate; [F23] says the auction rate on the date of "service of the order". The practical rule is unsettled.
- I found no 2026 change to US$25,000. Finance Act 7 of 2025 reportedly changes s 23 for mining companies only [S1]; an August 2026 article still uses US$25,000 [S3].
- Voluntary registration below the line is possible (s 23(3)). It can be refused without a fixed place, proper records or a bank account, or after past failures (s 23(7)). [A]

### 3. Start date

- Apply within 30 days of becoming liable; an incomplete application does not count (s 23(2)). [A]
- If the Business applies, it starts "with effect from such date as the Commissioner may determine" (s 23(4)(a)). If it does not, it starts from when it **first became liable**, unless a later date is allowed (s 23(4)(b)). [A]
- ZIMRA's online form guidance gives the effective date as "The first day of the next month after reaching of threshold" [Z1]. Use the date ZIMRA confirms, not the application date. It may be earlier than the day the Business is told.
- *Inference:* in that gap the Business may owe VAT but have no VAT number or fiscal device. Ask ZIMRA promptly and set money aside.

### 4. Time of supply

Normal rule, s 8(1) as replaced by Act 1 of 2019 [A]: the earliest of

1. "an invoice is issued" (s 2: "a document notifying an obligation to make payment", so its heading does not decide it);
2. "any payment of consideration is received";
3. moveable goods removed from the place of sale, or possession of immovable goods taken;
4. service performed.

Exceptions that matter:

- **Deposits** (s 2, "consideration"): a deposit "whether refundable or not" "shall not be considered as payment made for the supply unless and until the supplier applies the deposit as consideration for the supply or such deposit is forfeited" [A]. This fits the app's meaning of Deposit and Applied. Calling a part-payment a Deposit does not make it one.
- **Stage payments** (s 8(3)(b)): where goods are supplied progressively, or goods or services are supplied directly in construction, repair, improvement or erection, and the agreement makes payment due in instalments **for progress**, each stage is a separate supply. Its time is the earliest of payment due, payment received, or an invoice for only that payment. Performance is not a trigger here. [A] *Inference:* fencing, gate, borehole and plumbing contracts with agreed stages can fall here.
- Connected persons have a special rule (s 8(2)(a)). [A]

VAT is on the **invoice basis** (s 14): the Business can owe it before the Customer pays. A payments basis is possible only if regulations allow it and the Business applies; I did not read those regulations. [A]

### 5. Which Invoices carry VAT around registration

*R* is the effective registration date. Section 82 covers only the 2004 start of VAT. The table is **inference** from ss 2, 6 and 8; "firm" means close to the text.

| Situation | VAT? | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Work done and Invoice issued before R, paid after | No; later payment does not move the supply | Firm |
| Invoice issued before R, part-paid, balance after | No | Firm for one-off work |
| Invoice before R for work to be done after R | No on literal s 8(1)(a) | Weak. Do not invoice early to avoid VAT: s 77 lets ZIMRA undo schemes |
| Work fully finished before R (not stage-billed), Invoice after | No; performance, removal or possession came first | Good, but keep proof of completion |
| Deposit held at R; work and Invoice after | Yes, on the **full** price | Firm if truly a Deposit |
| Deposit Applied before R | Literally counts as payment before R | Uncertain if the work is after R |
| Real part-payment (not Deposit) before R, all work after | "Any payment" literally fixes the supply before R | Uncertain whether for the whole price |
| Single-price work under way, no invoice or payment, finished after R | Yes on the whole price unless apportioned | Uncertain |
| Agreed stage due, paid or invoiced before R | No on that stage | Good |
| Agreed stage first due, paid or invoiced after R | Yes | Good; ask about apportionment if that stage's work was before R |
| Work, payment and Invoice wholly after R | Yes if standard-rated | Firm |

**Apportionment:** s 73(2) splits work spanning the date tax is "imposed" or its rate changes, and does not tax work wholly provided before tax is imposed. It reads as a rule for changes in the law; whether a trader's own registration counts is not stated. The same doubt affects s 72, which lets a registered operator add newly imposed VAT to an accepted price unless a written agreement says otherwise. [A] Do not rely on either without advice.

**Quotations:** a Quotation is not a supply. After R its price must include tax and say so (s 70(1)). If an accepted price says nothing, s 69 treats it as **including** VAT, so the Business may bear it: US$1,000 contains 1,000 x 15.5 / 115.5 = US$134.20. *Inference:* replace unaccepted Quotations with a Revision, and before R state in writing what happens to the price if the Business registers.

**Credit and Receipts:** *inference:* Credit tied to no supply is not payment for a supply until Applied. A Receipt should not add VAT a second time.

### 6. Which supplies

- Standard-rated unless exempt, zero-rated or special [Z2]. I found no special rule for fencing, gates, welding, boreholes or plumbing in ss 10 and 11, so they are **standard-rated by default**, parts and labour alike. Regulation exemptions are the caveat below.
- Zero rate (s 10) is mainly exports and related services. Regulation zero-rating was repealed from 1 January 2024. [A, Z2]
- Exemptions (s 11) include letting a dwelling, education, medical and financial services, and supplies prescribed in regulations. Working **on** a house or school is not the same thing. [A]
- SI 15 of 2024 exempts, among others, piped domestic water and agricultural equipment in **listed commodity codes** [S4]. I have not seen the code list or later amendments: check agricultural pumps and equipment.
- 2025 additions reportedly cover prescribed agricultural goods and services, and electrification funded by the Rural Electrification Fund [S1]. Ordinary electrical installation work does not look like the latter (*inference*).
- If a price covers taxable and other things, only the part attributable to the taxable supply is taxed (s 9(21)). [A]

### 7. VAT claimed back

- Claim VAT on inputs for taxable supplies, with a valid tax invoice or import document (ss 2, 15, 16), generally within 12 months. Mixed use is apportioned; 90% taxable use may count as all (s 16(1)). Entertainment is mostly refused (s 16(2)). [A, Z2]
- **Stock bought before registration:** s 17(4)(b) appears to allow a deduction when goods or services on which VAT was charged, with no earlier deduction, are later applied to taxable supplies, using the lesser of cost and market value. Whether ZIMRA accepts it for a late registrant is unconfirmed. Keep supplier VAT invoices and a dated stock list. [A; application is inference] Section 19 covers pre-**incorporation** costs, a different case.
- From 2026, fiscal invoices feed claims automatically, and only those "Valid" on FDMS with correct buyer details count [Z4]. Suppliers need the Business's correct details.

### 8. Tax invoice

- Issue within **30 days of supply**, only one per supply; a duplicate is marked "copy" (s 20(1)). [A, Z5]
- Contents (s 20(4)) [A]: prominent "fiscal tax invoice"; supplier name, address and registration number; recipient name and address, plus number if registered; serial number and date; description; quantity or volume; and either value, tax and total, **or** (tax fraction method) total with the tax amount or a statement that it includes tax and the rate.
- From 1 January 2026, reportedly also TIN and a QR or verification code [S2]. The definition reportedly requires a fiscal device, transmission to FDMS and a valid check [S1], consistent with [Z4]. Virtual devices or software are reportedly allowed [S1].
- All registered points of sale must use compliant fiscal devices [Z4]. Failing to issue a fiscal invoice or receipt is an offence, including for someone merely **required** to be registered (s 63A). [A]
- s 20(5) waives a tax invoice up to US$10; check the s 63A fiscal duty separately. [A]
- Keep records for 6 years (s 57). [A]

### 9. Cancellations, credit notes and discounts

- After registration, if a supply is cancelled or fundamentally changed, its price is changed by agreement, or goods return, adjust the VAT (s 21). Too much VAT on the tax invoice needs a **credit note**; too little, a **debit note**. [A] Marking the tax invoice Cancelled is not enough in those cases. For a pure issuing mistake, the fiscal device procedure was not established.
- Credit note contents (s 21(3)): "credit note"; supplier name, address and registration number; recipient name and address unless the stated exception applies; date; reduction in value and VAT (or VAT-inclusive reduction and tax or rate); brief reason; enough to identify the transaction. [A]
- Before deducting VAT borne by an unregistered Customer, the Business must repay or credit it (s 21(2)). [A]
- A credit note is not the app's **Credit** (money held).
- *Inference:* a discount agreed before invoicing reduces the taxed price. One agreed later is a price change needing a credit note. A prompt-payment discount clearly stated on the tax invoice needs no credit note (s 21(3) proviso C). [A]

### 10. Bad debts

- If VAT was accounted for in a return and the debt is **written off** as irrecoverable, deduct the matching VAT share (s 22(1)). Pay that share back if money is later recovered (s 22(3)). A slow payer alone does not qualify, and an old Invoice without VAT gives nothing. [A]
- As a buyer, VAT claimed on supplies still unpaid after 12 months is generally payable back (s 22(4)). [A]

### 11. Rounding and currency

- s 71: tax is calculated "to the nearest cent giving advantage to the taxpayer, as the Commissioner may from time to time prescribe". [A] Line versus total rounding and half-cents were not established; match the fiscal device.
- VAT on a tax-exclusive price: price x 15.5%. VAT inside a total: total x 15.5 / 115.5 (s 2, "tax fraction").
- Foreign-currency price: tax is paid in that currency. Local-currency price: tax may be paid in local or foreign currency (s 38(4), (4a)). [A] Invoicing in one currency and being paid in another needs confirmation.

### 12. Returns and rate history

- Default is a two-month period (category A or B). Category C is monthly, including by direction when taxable supplies exceed or are likely to exceed US$240,000 (s 27). [A]
- The old 25th deadline became the 15th in 2025 [F24]. Current ZIMRA notice: return by the **10th**, payment by the **15th** [Z4]. ZIMRA's Mechanics page still says 25th.

| From | Standard rate | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Before 2020 (start not checked) | 15% | [S5] secondary |
| 1 January 2020 | 14.5% | [S5] secondary |
| 1 January 2023 | 15% | [S5] secondary |
| 1 January 2026 | 15.5% | ZIMRA [Z3] |

ZIMRA keeps the old rate where s 8 places a supply before the change [Z3].

## Worked examples

Assume R = **1 March 2027**, standard-rated work, unconnected Customer, rate still 15.5%, price **US$1,000 before VAT**, and one-off billing unless stated. All follow the inferences above.

**A. Supply before.** Done and invoiced 20 February, paid 10 March: US$1,000, no VAT. Done 20 February but invoiced 5 March: still no VAT on the best reading, because performance came first.

**B. Supply after.** Work 8 March, Invoice 10 March: US$1,000 + 155 = **US$1,155**, on a fiscal tax invoice within 30 days of supply.

**C. Deposit held.** US$400 Deposit on 10 February, not Applied; work and Invoice in March. VAT is on all US$1,000: total 1,155, Deposit Applied 400, balance **US$755**. If the accepted price cannot be increased, the total stays 1,000 (865.80 + 134.20 VAT) and the balance is **US$600**.

**D. Part-paid Invoice.** Finished and invoiced for US$1,000 on 15 February; 400 paid then and 600 on 20 March: no VAT. The balance stays US$600.

**E. Work under way.** 60% done before R; finished 20 March.

- One price, no earlier invoice or payment, no apportionment: VAT **US$155**, total US$1,155.
- Same, if s 73(2) apportionment is accepted: VAT on 400 = **US$62**, total US$1,062.
- Agreed stages, US$600 invoiced before R and US$400 due and invoiced after: VAT **US$62** on the second stage.

The first two are competing answers, not choices. Get advice before issuing.

## What the app needs (inference)

- No VAT or VAT wording until ZIMRA's effective date and VAT number are entered.
- A time-of-supply date for each Invoice or stage. Warn on Jobs, Deposits and unpaid Invoices crossing R, and let a reviewed VAT decision be recorded. Never rewrite Issued Documents.
- Dated rates, with the rate and treatment stored per line; VAT-inclusive totals; credit and debit notes; fiscal device integration or hand-off.
- A rolling 12-month taxable-supplies figure and a forward warning before US$25,000.
